Most symptoms that make people worry about cancer turn out to be caused by something far less serious. A lump, persistent fatigue or a change in bowel habits (even if it seems concerning) does not automatically indicate cancer. 

What matters is whether a symptom gets worse over time or is associated with other warning signs. Early cancers may not cause pain, which makes it essential to pay attention to any symptoms that persist or worsen. Ignoring such symptoms may allow the cancer to progress to nearby tissues or lymph nodes by the time it is detected. 

This page explains which symptoms need medical attention, what else might be causing them, and when it is time to see a doctor. 

## The symptoms that genuinely warrant assessment 

* **A lump anywhere in the body that is new and persistent:** First you should know that not every lump or temporary swelling means that it is cancer. Sometimes something as common as a cold may cause your lymph nodes to swell when your body is fighting the infection. The swelling usually gets better as you start to recover from the infection.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt) However if you notice a lump or swelling that has been there for weeks and is continuing to grow, it might point to something serious. So, if you notice a lump or even a swelling in your neck, armpit, private areas, breast, or your abdomen that has persisted for weeks, you should get it checked by a doctor.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)   
* **A mouth ulcer or a white patch/red patch that has not healed:** Mouth ulcers are usually common and normally heal on their own within a week or two. However, if an ulcer does not heal for a few weeks or if you notice a persistent white or red patch anywhere in the mouth, you should get it checked by a doctor or dentist.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms) These changes may have several other causes but they can also be one of the early signs of oral cancer so it is important that you do not ignore them. This is particularly important for men in India where oral cancer is a major concern. In 2024, cancers of the lip and oral cavity accounted for 20.4% of all new cancer cases among men, making them the leading cancer type in men.[3](https://gco.iarc.who.int/today/)   
* **Persistent cough for more than a few weeks:** A cough is a very common ailment that is usually caused by a cold, infection, or another condition that may not be very serious. However, if your cough persists for longer than a few weeks or is worsening (and is accompanied by a croaky voice or hoarseness), it should not be ignored.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt),[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)   
* **A persistent or unexplained change in bowel habits**: An unexplained change in bowel habits, like being constipated frequently or needing to pass stools more often than usual, especially if it is persistent, should not be ignored. You should also see a doctor if you are experiencing bloating that occurs frequently or bothers you almost every day.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)    
* **Difficulty swallowing:** Pain or burning sensation when swallowing or feeling as though food is getting stuck in your throat/chest may sometimes signal an underlying problem.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms) So if you have persistent difficulty or pain when swallowing, you should consult your doctor.   
* **Persistent heartburn or indigestion:** Occasional heartburn or indigestion may occur after a spicy meal or drinking alcohol. However, if you experience these symptoms frequently and find them particularly painful or notice that they are not improving, you should speak to your doctor.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)   
* **Unexplained bleeding or bruising**: Noticing bleeding or bruising, especially when you have not injured yourself, should be checked by a doctor. This includes blood in your stool (which may appear bright red or dark and tarry), urine, vomit or vaginal discharge (bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause), coughing up blood and a bloody nipple discharge. Remember, even if the amount is small, do not ignore it.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt),[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)    
* **Unexplained weight loss or appetite loss:** Small changes in weight are normal. However, if you notice that you are losing a noticeable amount of weight without trying, it is important to speak to your doctor. Similarly, if you have lost your appetite and it does not improve, get it checked by a doctor.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)  
* **Skin changes:** A cut or sore that does not heal (even if it is painless) should be checked by a doctor. You should also pay attention to any new or unusual changes in your skin or nails. These include patches that change colour or shape. Most moles are harmless, but a new mole or an existing mole that changes in size, shape, or colour or becomes itchy, crusty, or starts bleeding should be examined. Yellowing of the skin or the whites of the eyes (jaundice) also needs medical attention.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)   
* **Persistent unexplained fatigue or pain:** Feeling tired can be caused by many things including poor sleep and stress. However, always feeling tired without any reason that does not seem to improve with rest should be discussed with your doctor. Similarly, unexplained pain or an ache that does not go away and keeps getting worse or changes over time should not be ignored.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)  
* **Night sweats or unexplained fever:** Night sweats and fever may sometimes occur due to infections, certain medications or hormonal changes. But if you experience heavy night sweats that soak your clothes or bedding or a fever that has no clear cause or keeps coming back, you should consult your doctor.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)    
* **Persistent or unexplained breathlessness:** Sometimes feeling out of breath after physical activity can be normal. However, if you become breathless more often than usual and experience it at rest or notice it while walking normally, you should speak to your doctor.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)

## Health conditions that can mimic cancer symptoms 

Tuberculosis (TB) may sometimes mimic cancer symptoms. This may occur more commonly in India due to its high TB burden.[11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35141090/) Nodal TB may resemble [lymphoma,](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/blood-cancer/lymphoma/) abdominal TB may resemble [bowel cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/colorectal-cancer/), and pulmonary TB may resemble [lung cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/lung-cancer/) on a chest x-ray.[12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5450601/),[13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9129883/),[14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5609136/) Other such examples include: 

* Fibroadenomas may mimic [breast cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/breast-cancer/).[15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20804869/)    
* Lipomas may mimic soft tissue sarcoma.[16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42598045/)    
* Thyroid nodules are benign in most cases.[17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32351856/)    
* Gallstones, fatty liver and reflux account for most upper abdominal discomfort.[18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4565319/),[19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1121646/) 

Although most symptoms are more likely to have a noncancerous cause, it is important not to make this assumption without proper assessment. A medical checkup can help identify the cause and determine whether further tests are needed. If you are unsure about a symptom, explore our page, [Is It Cancer?](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/symptoms/is-it-cancer/) which addresses the most common questions one at a time. 

## Red-flag combinations 

Sometimes, one symptom may not be concerning by itself; however, when certain symptoms occur together, they indicate the need for medical evaluation. Some common red-flag symptoms include: 

* Painless jaundice, feeling full quickly while eating (early satiety), and weight loss.[20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32104620/)   
* Persistent cough with blood-streaked sputum and weight loss.[21](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/haemoptysis-coughing-up-blood)   
* Bloating with early satiety, pelvic/abdominal pain and urinary urgency.[22](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ovarian-cancer/symptoms/)   
* Change in bowel habit with rectal bleeding or low blood counts.[23](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/colon-rectal-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/signs-and-symptoms.html)   
* Painless neck swelling/lump with night sweats, fever, itchy skin and weight loss[.24](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/non-hodgkin-lymphoma/symptoms/)   
* Bone pain with a lump/swelling, fractures or difficulty in moving.[25](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bone-cancer/symptoms/)  

## Early symptoms versus late symptoms 

Early cancer symptoms are often mild and easy to overlook. These can include symptoms such as a small lump, a mouth patch, slight bleeding or a change in normal habits. As the disease progresses, symptoms may become more clearly indicative of cancer. These can include unexplained weight loss, severe fatigue, persistent pain, jaundice or breathlessness.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt),[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms) So the important thing is to not wait until a symptom becomes severe to get it checked. If a symptom is persistent, keeps getting worse, or has no obvious cause, speak to your doctor. 

## Variations by sex and site 

Cancer symptoms vary depending on the type of cancer and the organ affected. Some changes to watch out for may include: 

* **In women:** Some signs that may be indicative of cancer in women include:[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms),[26](https://www.cdc.gov/gynecologic-cancer/symptoms/index.html),[27](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/symptoms)   
> > > * Unusual vaginal bleeding (between periods, after sex, or after menopause) or discharge.   
> > > * Bloating, abdominal or back pain and early satiety or difficulty eating.   
> > > * Breast lump, changes in the nipple such as discharge other than breast milk or flattening of the nipple, skin changes such as redness, itching or tingling on the nipple or around it. 

**Note:** Only 0.9% of Indian women aged 30 to 49 years have ever had a breast examination.[28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39629359/) So, noticing and reporting any changes as soon as possible can help identify the problem early. Check our guide for more details on [common cancer symptoms and warning signs in women](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-common-cancer-symptoms-and-warning-signs-in-women/). 

* **In men:** Some signs that may be indicative of cancer in men include:   
> > > * A painless lump or swelling in a testicle; pain, discomfort or unusual feeling in the testicles; or sudden fluid build-up in the scrotum.[29](https://www.cancer.gov/types/testicular/symptoms)   
> > > * Problems while urinating (such as not being able to empty the bladder, leakage after urinating, slow stream of urine), or blood in the urine/semen.[30](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000380.htm)   
> > > * Mouth ulcers or other oral changes in men using tobacco in the form of khaini, gutkha, zarda, snuff, or betel quid.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms),[31](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35281158/)   
* **In children:** Some signs that may be indicative of cancer and are observed in children include:   
> > > * Fever, pale skin, easy bruising, bone or joint pain, and painless lumps.[32](https://www.cancer.gov/types/leukemia/patient/child-all-treatment-pdq)   
> > > * A white glow in the eyes/eye seen in photographs taken with flash or eyes that do not align.[33](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001030.htm)   
> > > * Headaches that are worse on waking, sleeping, coughing, repeated vomiting and changes in behaviour, concentration and memory.[34](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000768.htm)   
* **By site:** Sometimes tumours affecting different organs may show slightly different symptoms:   
> > > * Tumours in hollow organs (like the colon) may cause obstruction and bleeding.[35](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/bowel-obstruction)    
> > > * Tumours in solid organs (like the liver) do not show symptoms until they press on an internal structure.[36](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24778418/),[37](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1534580710002480)   
> > > * Tumours near a nerve may cause pain or weakness early.[38](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18312658/)   
> > > * Tumours in the marrow may lead to anaemia, infection, and bleeding.[39](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7912724/)   
  To know more, see [types of cancer, symptoms and early signs](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/diagnostic-types-of-cancer-symptoms-early-signs-diagnosis-more/) or [early warning signs of cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/diagnostic-early-warning-signs-of-cancer-you-should-know/). 

## When to see a doctor 

Cancer warning signs can resemble symptoms of other conditions, so medical assessment is needed to identify the cause. Contact your doctor if you notice: 

* New lump, breast shape changes, skin dimpling or discoloration, swelling, rash, itching, or unusual nipple discharge or inversion.[27](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/symptoms)   
* A painless lump, dull ache or any changes in the testicles.[29](https://www.cancer.gov/types/testicular/symptoms)   
* Difficulty or pain when swallowing.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)   
* Blood in the stool, urine, or a change in bowel habits.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)  
* Painless swelling in the neck, armpit or groin that does not go away.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt),[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)  
* Mouth or throat sore with a red or white patch or cough and hoarse voice that does not improve for a longer duration.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)  
* Bloating, pelvic pain, unusual bleeding or any other unusual gynaecological symptoms.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms),[26](https://www.cdc.gov/gynecologic-cancer/symptoms/index.html)   
* A new mole or sore that does not heal in a week.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)

**Note:** See your doctor immediately if you cough up or vomit blood, have a seizure for the first time, develop a sudden severe headache with vomiting, experience new one-sided weakness or numbness, become breathless at rest, or develop rapid swelling in any body part, fever with a stiff neck, or severe pain anywhere in the body.[40](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000593.htm) 

If you are unsure when to see a doctor, an [online doctor consultation](https://pharmeasy.in/online-doctor-consultation/) can help you understand what to do next and whether you need an in-person examination.

## Frequently asked questions 

▾ What are the first signs of cancer? 

The signs vary depending on the type of cancer and where it develops. Possible warning signs include unexplained weight loss, unusual bleeding or vaginal discharge, prolonged cough, hoarseness, changes in bladder or bowel habits, problems with swallowing, changes in skin, such as yellowing or redness, extreme tiredness and persistent pain without an obvious cause.[1](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/5-symptoms-of-cancer-you-might-be-ignoring-but-shouldnt),[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms)

▾ Are cancer lumps painful? 

Cancer lumps are mostly painless; however, sometimes they may be painful. Cancers like Hodgkin lymphoma may cause painless swollen glands.[41](https://www.cancer.gov/types/lymphoma/patient/adult-hodgkin-treatment-pdq) A soft tissue sarcoma lump may become painful as it grows and presses on nearby nerves or muscles.[42](https://www.cancer.gov/types/soft-tissue-sarcoma/patient/adult-soft-tissue-treatment-pdq)  

▾ Can a lump on the neck be cancer? 

Yes, but neck lumps can also result from infections, cysts, neck muscle injury, thyroid diseases, or allergic reactions. Most neck lumps in adults are not cancerous, but a doctor should check a new or unexplained lump.[4](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003098.htm) 

▾ What cancer causes itchy skin? 

Bile duct cancer may also cause itching, particularly when it blocks the flow of bile.[43](https://www.cancer.gov/types/liver/hp/bile-duct-treatment-pdq) However, itching can have other causes, including liver, thyroid, kidney, endocrine or metabolic problems, so it does not confirm cancer.[5](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/itchy-skin),[44](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40918704/) 

▾ Are varicose veins a sign of cancer? 

Varicose veins are usually caused by weak or damaged vein walls and valves, which allow blood to collect in the veins. Contact your doctor if they suddenly worsen or you develop a sore and seek emergency care if they bleed.[6](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001109.htm),[45](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/varicose-veins) 

▾ What are the symptoms of digestive system cancer? 

Possible symptoms include difficulty swallowing, abdominal discomfort or indigestion, changes in bowel habits or blood in the stool, and jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes).[46](https://www.cancer.gov/types/colorectal/patient/colon-treatment-pdq),[47](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stomach-cancer/symptoms/),[48](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/liver-cancer/symptoms/) These symptoms can also have other causes, so a doctor needs to assess them. 

▾ What are the symptoms of small intestine cancer? 

Possible symptoms may include unexplained weight loss, cramps or pain in the middle of the abdomen, blood in the stools and a lump in the abdomen.[49](https://www.cancer.gov/types/small-intestine/patient/small-intestine-treatment-pdq) Other conditions can cause similar symptoms, so medical assessment is needed to identify the cause 

▾ How long should I wait before seeing a doctor? 

See a doctor if an unexplained symptom persists for a long time or does not improve or worsens.[2](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms) However, you should get checked immediately if you notice a growing lump, unusual bleeding and any other severe symptoms. 

▾ Does having several symptoms mean it is more likely to be cancer? 

Symptoms that occur together can be more concerning than a single symptom, especially when they are linked, such as jaundice with unexplained weight loss or a persistent cough with weight loss and blood.[20](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32104620/),[21](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/haemoptysis-coughing-up-blood) However, other conditions can cause these symptoms too. So, having several symptoms together does not automatically mean cancer, but it is a reason to see a doctor.
