Many cancers can be cured, especially when doctors diagnose them early and provide the right treatment. However, the chances of a cure vary from person to person. It depends on the ​     ​​[type of cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/)​, how much it has spread, what the disease biology is  and the person’s overall health.[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer)

According to the ICMR-NCDIR National Cancer Registry Programme, India recorded an estimated 14,61,427 cancer cases in India in 2022\. The cases were projected to be 12.8% higher in 2025 compared to 2020\.[2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36510887/) However, a cancer diagnosis does not always mean that the disease cannot be cured. In some cases, treatment may not completely remove the cancer but can slow its growth and keep it under control for a long time.[3](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/making-treatment-decisions/if-cancer-treatments-stop-working.html) 

This article explains the difference between curing cancer, controlling its growth and relieving symptoms. It also covers how doctors decide on a treatment plan and the role of different cancer treatments. ## What "cure" actually means in cancer care 

Doctors are very careful when they use the word “cure” when it comes to cancer. Even when treatment works well, tests cannot always confirm that every cancer cell has disappeared. Doctors usually confirm there is no detectable cancer on examination, blood tests, and scans, which can be described as complete remission or no evidence of disease.[4](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/understanding-cancer/can-cancer-be-cured.html) 

If the cancer remains in remission (absent) for a long time, for eg. 5 years, and the chance of it returning becomes very low, oncologists may sometimes call it “cured”.[4](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/understanding-cancer/can-cancer-be-cured.html) However, the time needed to reach this point varies by cancer type.

Some cancers are unlikely to return when several years have passed without signs of the disease. In contrast, certain cancers like hormone-sensitive ​​​[breast cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/breast-cancer/)​ can return even several years after treatment. This is why long-term follow-up may continue for a decade or longer.[5](https://www.bcrf.org/about-breast-cancer/breast-cancer-recurrence/)

Doctors explain cancer treatment based on its main goal: 

* **Curative treatment:** Aims to remove or destroy the cancer completely.[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer)  
* **Cancer** **control**: Aims to shrink the cancer, slow its growth or prevent it from worsening, helping the person stay well for as long as possible.[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer)  
* **Symptom relief**: Aims on reducing/easing pain, breathlessness, bleeding, or blockages caused by cancer. 

These goals may change as the person's condition and response to treatment change.[6](https://www.cdc.gov/cancer-survivors/patients/treatments.html) For example, treatment that initially aims to cure cancer may later focus on controlling the disease or relieving symptoms. In some cases, the treatment goal may change in the other direction if circumstances improve.

## How your treatment plan is decided 

### What doctors consider when planning treatment? 

Doctors consider several factors before deciding on a cancer treatment plan. These include the type of cancer confirmed by a biopsy, its stage (how far it has spread), biological markers produced by cancer cells, and your overall health.[11](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/diagnosis-staging/staging.html) 

In many cancer centres, specialists work together to decide on the most suitable treatment for each patient. The team may include: 

* **Surgeon:** A doctor who performs operations to remove tumours or other affected tissues.   
* **Medical oncologist:** A doctor who treats cancer using medicines, such as chemotherapy and targeted therapy.   
* **Radiation oncologist:** A doctor who uses radiation to treat cancer.   
* **Pathologist:** A specialist who examines tissue samples under a microscope to help confirm the diagnosis and identify the type of cancer.   
* **Radiologist:** A doctor who examines medical images, such as X-rays, computed tomography (CT) scans and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans, to assess the cancer.[12](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/preparing-for-treatment/cancer-care-team.html) 

Together, these specialists review the patient's test results and discuss treatment options based on the type of cancer, its stage and the patient's overall health. 

If you are receiving treatment at a smaller cancer centre, you can ask whether a team of specialists has reviewed your case together in a tumour board meeting. You can also check whether the centre is part of the National Cancer Grid (NCG), a network of cancer centres across India that works to improve the quality and consistency of cancer care.[13](https://www.ncgindia.org/about) 

### **How cancer tests guide treatment?** 

Certain tests look for specific features, such as genes, proteins or substances, that give information about cancer cells. These are called biomarker tests, and their results can help doctors choose treatments that are more likely to work for a type of cancer. For example, hormone receptor and HER2 tests can guide treatment for breast cancer.[14](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/diagnosis-staging/tests/biomarker-tests.html) EGFR and ALK tests can help guide treatment for some lung cancers. Mismatch repair tests can help guide treatment for certain colorectal and endometrial cancers, while BCR-ABL testing helps guide treatment for chronic myeloid leukaemia, a type of blood cancer.[15](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-list) 

Ask your doctor which tests were performed on your biopsy sample and whether any results are still pending. In some cases, doctors may need these results before finalising or adjusting the treatment plan.

### How doctors combine treatments 

Doctors may combine two or more treatments or give them in a planned sequence. For example, treatment for stage II breast cancer may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and hormone therapy, depending on the tumour's characteristics and the patient's needs.[22](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/treatment/by-stage) These choices are based on international guidelines made specifically for every stage and type of cancer.

## Supportive care: palliative care and side effects 

Palliative care is sometimes mistaken for the end of cancer treatment.[34](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/9/3/e024248) Side effects are also mistaken for proof that treatment is working.[18](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/chemotherapy) Neither assumption is correct, and both beliefs can influence the care people accept. 

### What palliative care actually is? 

Palliative care provides specialist help with symptoms such as pain, breathlessness, nausea, appetite loss, and anxiety. It can be given alongside cancer treatment, rather than replacing it.[10](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet) 

According to a study published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (2010), in patients with metastatic non-small cell [lung cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/lung-cancer/), those who received early palliative care alongside standard cancer care had better quality-of-life scores and fewer symptoms of depression. 

They were shown to be less likely to receive intensive treatment at the end of life. They also lived longer on average, with a median survival of 11.6 months compared with 8.9 months in the other group.[35](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1000678) 

These findings deserve attention because research in India has identified beliefs that associate palliative care with giving up.[34](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/9/3/e024248) 

According to WHO, only about 14% of people who need palliative care receive it.[36](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391806558_Confronting_global_inequities_in_palliative_care) In a retrospective analysis on morphine needs and consumption in cancer patients globally, it was found that in 2017, only 19% of countries, mainly in high-income groups, had sufficient morphine levels to meet the calculated need for cancer pain relief.[37](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10021698/) This unequal access helps explain why cancer pain remains untreated in low- and middle-income countries. 

 

### Side effects: what they mean and what they do not 

Almost all [cancer treatments can cause side effects](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/side-effects/).[18](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/chemotherapy) Many can be managed, some may be stopped from developing, and many improve after treatment ends. Modern anti-sickness medicines, growth factor injections for low white blood cell counts, mouth care protocols[10](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet),[39](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/mouth-throat#ways-to-prevent-mouth-and-dental-problems) and scalp cooling (approved treatment for chemotherapy induced hair loss)[40](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10137043/) have substantially improved the treatment experience over the past few years. 

During treatment, routine blood tests help the team monitor your health by checking how well your organs are working and identifying possible treatment side effects, in addition to monitoring the cancer.[38](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/patient-education/chemo-and-you) The blood count can indicate whether the bone marrow has recovered before the next cycle. 

The treating team sometimes request a C-reactive protein (CRP) test, which is a general measure of inflammation in the body, when they think the infection is present. However, CRP is not a specific cancer test; it can rise with inflammation or infection in any part of the body, and cannot, on its own, confirm or rule out cancer.[41](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/c-reactive-protein-crp-test/) Your treatment team will decide which tests you exactly need and how often they should be done. 

One more misunderstanding needs a clear explanation. Side effects do not show how efficiently chemotherapy is managing your cancer.[18](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/chemotherapy) Completing six chemotherapy cycles with only mild tiredness does not mean the treatment is weak. Similarly, experiencing severe nausea does not mean the treatment is more effective. The side effects and management page explains this further, including what to do if problems arise at night. 

 

## Paying for cancer treatment in India 

Cancer treatment is not just a medical concern but also a financial one, because financial difficulty can lead people to stop treatment midway. More than 75% of cancer-affected households in India had to pay bills that severely affected their financial conditions, called catastrophic health expenditures (CHE).42 A study at Tata Memorial Centre in Mumbai, conducted among around 500 breast cancer patients, found that 84.2%  of patients' households were affected by CHE with 72.4% distress financing, like borrowing money or selling assets, to pay for the treatment.43 

The following measures may help reduce these costs: 

* Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY scheme covers the money needed for managing cancer for qualified families at empanelled hospitals.[44](https://supportbeta.nha.gov.in/kb/faq.php?id=57)   
* Health Minister's Cancer Patient Fund under Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi provides one-time assistance of up to ₹15 lakh for qualified patients receiving treatment at Regional Cancer Centres and government cancer facilities.[45](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2154159&reg=48&lang=2) This offers an additional route to financial support for eligible candidates under PM-JAY for treatment that is not covered under the scheme.   
* Medicine price regulation can reduce costs. In February 2019, NPPA introduced trade margin rationalisation for 42 non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines, covering 526 brands. For one brand, the price of anticancer drug erlotinib 150 mg fell from ₹9,999 to ₹891.79.[46](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1670707&reg=48&lang=2) 

   
Contact your treating team's medical social worker to check these three options before you  borrow money from others.   
 

## When to see a doctor 

Seek immediate medical care for the following: 

* A temperature of 38°C (100.5°F) or higher while receiving chemotherapy[47](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/infection)   
* Bleeding that does not stop    
*  sudden breathlessness   
* Severe vomiting   
* New weakness or numbness in the hands or legs   
* Difficulty controlling your bladder or not being able to pass urine[48](https://www.cancer.nsw.gov.au/about-cancer/document-library/cancer-treatment-side-effects-a-guide-for-aborigin/side-effects-from-anticancer-medicines-immune-rela) 

Consult your oncology team within days if you have: 

* Vomiting that stops you from keeping fluids down   
* Persistent or bloody diarrhoea   
* Mouth pain that makes you unable to eat or a new mouth sore   
* New swelling or pain at the IV site   
* Yellowing of the eyes (jaundice)[49](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000012.htm) 

If you need help in understanding a report, clarifying a prescription or obtaining an informed second opinion before committing to a plan, an [online doctor appointment](https://pharmeasy.in/online-doctor-consultation/) can provide access to qualified medical advice in between hospital visits. However, it is not meant that you should replace your oncologist.

### What drives the cost 

There are no rupee figures here. Cost varies depending on the city, hospital, stage, regimen and whether the treatment is done in a public, trust-run or private set-up. So, a relatively reliable way to make an estimate would be by understanding the way each component of the treatment is billed. 

| Component  | How it is billed  | What changes the total  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Surgery**  | One-time procedure  | The operation type, the hospital grade, length of stay and any complications.51  |
| **Radiotherapy**  | Per fraction, billed as a course  | The method and quantity of fractions. Delivered daily for several weeks, commonly as day care.[52](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/radiation-therapy/external-beam)  |
| **Chemotherapy**  | Per cycle  | The regimen, the number of cycles, day-care charges, and the supportive medicines given alongside to manage side effects.  |
| **Targeted therapy and immunotherapy**  | Charges may apply per dose, treatment cycle, or month, depending on the medicine and billing arrangement.  | The specific medicine, dose, frequency, and duration of treatment may affect the overall expense. The treatment period varies according to the cancer, medicine used and response to treatment.53  |
| **Diagnosis and staging**  | Usually charged separately for each test or procedure.  | Imaging, biopsy, histopathology and, increasingly, molecular testing that decides which drug is appropriate.[54](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/hp/breast-treatment-pdq)  |
| **Supportive care**  | May be charged per cycle, per hospital visit or whenever needed.  | Anti-sickness medicines, growth factors, transfusions and the management of complications.[26](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK564367/),[32](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK559282/) These costs are often underestimated when planning.  |

 

The unit is more important than the headline figure as per-cycle cost multiplies by the number of cycles given, and in case of ongoing treatment, the monthly cost is continuous. . In India, about 75% of households with cancer experience catastrophic costs.[42](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40368978/) 

Figures are CMS fields and are not populated in this prototype. Full cost guidance and the schemes available in India are available on [cost and financial support](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/cost-and-support/). 

### Insurance in India- what to check 

There are several factors to consider here; your policy, your insurer, the hospital and the policy terms. All these determine whether any insurance applies to you. The list provided below can be used as a list of questions to be asked to the insurer before treatment. However, note that nothing mentioned below is a statement that your treatment is covered.  

| What to ask about  | Why it matters for cancer treatment  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Is it an indemnity policy or a cancer-specific plan?**  | In an indemnity health policy, hospital costs are covered. Under a cancer-specific or critical-illness plan, a lump sum payment is typically made when a specific stage is diagnosed. Many people hold one while thinking they hold the other, however these two behave differently.[55](https://irdai.gov.in/web/policy-holder/health-insurance)  |
| **Day-care treatment**  | Chemotherapy and radiotherapy are generally given without an overnight stay. Older policies only paid for hospital stays of 24 hours; most policies now specifically list day-care policies. Confirm that the insurer’s day care list contains the specific procedure.[55](https://irdai.gov.in/web/policy-holder/health-insurance)  |
| **Pre- and post-hospitalisation**  | Scans, biopsies and blood work before admission, and follow-up after discharge, are generally covered only when associated with the same claim and for a limited period of time. Records of receipt should be maintained.  |
| **Waiting periods**  | A cancer diagnosed during the initial waiting period of policy’s or before policy initiation is generally excluded. Waiting periods for pre-existing conditions and specific illnesses like cancer can differ from one another and may not be 30 days in all cases. It's important to cross-check the exact terms in the policy.  |
| **Sub-limits, co-pay, and room rent**  | The entire claim can be proportionately reduced by a room-rent cap in your policy. What you actually receive is altered by co-pay percentages and sub-limits for particular procedures.  |
| **Cashless or reimbursement**  | Pre-authorisation 48 to 72 hours ahead of admissions is needed for cashless insurance at hospitals that lie within the network. You need to make the payment first and claim later when not in the network.  |
| **Documents to keep from day one**  | The doctor’s prescription and treatment plan, policy number and card, the histopathology and staging reports, discharge summary, itemised hospital bill, pharmacy invoices and diagnostic reports. The most common cause of a cancer claim stall is missing histopathology.   |

## Frequently asked questions 

 

▾ **Can cancer be cured completely?** 

Many types of cancers can be cured completely. Early-stage breast, cervical, oral, colorectal, testicular and thyroid cancers, most childhood leukaemias and lymphomas, and several others are usually treated with an intention to cure. Curing cancer depends on type, stage and biology, and your oncologist can tell you which intent applies to your case[56](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/understanding-cancer/can-cancer-be-cured.html)

▾ **Which cancer treatment is the best one?** 

There is no single best treatment. The right treatment is the one that matches your tumour type, stage and biomarkers. A standard treatment for one type of cancer can be actively useless for another, which is why treatment plans vary even in patients with the same diagnosis label. 

▾ **Is chemotherapy always needed?** 

No. Chemotherapy may not always be needed for cancer. Treatment is usually based on the type and stage of cancer. Few people may need surgery, while others may need radiation therapy or other treatment approaches.[18](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/chemotherapy) 

▾**Does stage 4 mean that treatment is pointless?** 

Stage 4 cancer can mean different things for different cancers. Some stage 4 cancers may be cured also. Some patients with indolent cancers may live with their stage 4 disease for years on end without problems. For cancers in which the disease is advanced and the cancer has spread to distant organs, the treatment may focus on controlling the cancer rather than curing it. Still it can help slow its growth, ease symptoms and enable some people live longer with a better quality of life[57](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/breast-cancer/treatment/treatment-of-breast-cancer-by-stage/treatment-of-stage-iv-advanced-breast-cancer.html).

▾ **Does palliative care mean I’ll no longer live?** 

No. Palliative care is given in parallel with active treatment to control the symptoms. Evidence indicates that it improves quality of life. It can start on the day of diagnosis and continue while you are on chemotherapy or radiation.[10](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet)

**▾Is Ayurveda or homoeopathy helpful for cancer?** 

There is no reliable evidence that Ayurveda or homoeopathy can cure cancer. Some complementary approaches may help ease symptoms, but they should not replace proven cancer treatments, and patients should consult their oncologist before using them[58](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam).Inform your oncologist about any medicines you are taking, because some herbal supplements can interfere with chemotherapy medicines. 

▾ **Should I get a second opinion?** 

If you have any doubt, it's good to go for a second opinion. A professional oncologist will never object to you doing that. Carry your biopsy results and slides, all imaging test results, and the full pathology report. This is important, as a second opinion without original reports may sometimes be of limited value.[50](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/making-treatment-decisions/seeking-a-second-opinion.html)
