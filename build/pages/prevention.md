According to the World Health Organization (WHO) approximately 38% of cancers worldwide are preventable. However, the potential for prevention varies across different risk factors with each contributing differently to the overall cancer burden. WHO states that the major risk factors associated with cancer include tobacco use, alcohol consumption, high body mass index, infections, air pollution, an unhealthy diet, and physical inactivity[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer).

In India tobacco-related cancers were estimated to account for 27.1% of all cancer cases in 2020\. This highlights tobacco use as a major modifiable risk factor[2](https://www.who.int/india/news-room/detail/09-02-2021-india-loses-1-of-its-gdp-to-diseases-and-early-deaths-from-tobacco-use-finds-who-study). Infections also contribute substantially to the preventable cancer burden. In low- and lower-middle-income countries, cancer-causing infections including those caused by human papillomavirus (HPV) and hepatitis B and C viruses account for approximately 30% of cancer cases[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer). Vaccination (like the [cervical cancer vaccine](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/hpv/)), screening, and appropriate treatment of infections may help prevent some of these cancers[3](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cervical-cancer).

This page explains what can be changed to reduce cancer risk, what cannot, and where to go next. Keep in mind that prevention reduces risk but cannot guarantee that cancer will not occur. Even people who take every precaution can develop cancer, and this should never be seen as their fault.

**Where risk comes from.** Cancer risk comes from three main factors: what you inherit, what you are exposed to throughout life, and chance. Age, sex, and inherited gene changes shape your baseline risk[13](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/age),[14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40880847/),[15](https://www.cdc.gov/breast-ovarian-cancer-hereditary/causes/index.html),[18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6029678/). Exposures such as tobacco, infections, alcohol, workplace carcinogens, air pollution, and radiation can increase it[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer). Finally random errors can occur when cells divide and copy their DNA[17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497260/). 

Air pollution is an important cancer risk factor[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer), particularly for Indian’s,  who cannot easily control their exposure. It can increase lung cancer risk even in people who have never smoked. Learn more about [why air pollution can cause lung cancer in people who have never smoked](https://pharmeasy.in/qna/question/air-pollution-can-cause-lung-cancer-even-in-non-smokers/). 

## Tobacco: the single largest modifiable cause in India 

Tobacco use in India takes two main forms: smoking and smokeless tobacco. According to the Global Adult Tobacco Survey (GATS-2, 2016 to 2017), 28.6% of Indian adults use tobacco, with 21.4% using smokeless tobacco and 10.7% smoking. Khaini (11.2%) is more commonly used than bidi (7.7%)[19](https://ntcp.mohfw.gov.in/assets/document/surveys-reports-publications/Global-Adult-Tobacco-Survey-Second-Round-India-2016-2017.pdf). So, cessation advice must reflect both of India’s tobacco-use patterns. In India, tobacco cessation strategies include behavioral counselling, nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), such as nicotine gums and patches and medication, if needed. The National Tobacco Quit Line Services help individuals looking to quit smoking by providing free, confidential telephonic support and counselling[20](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Dinesh-Kansal-2/publication/360143725_A_study_on_the_effectiveness_of_nicotine_and_counselling_in_tobacoo_smoking_cessation/links/636a41e754eb5f547cb30f57/A-study-on-the-effectiveness-of-nicotine-and-counselling-in-tobacoo-smoking-cessation.pdf?origin=scientificContributions).  

Health advice focused mainly on cigarettes and lung cancer may overlook smokeless tobacco users, including those who use khaini. GATS-2 found that 48.8% of smokers were advised to quit by a doctor, compared with just 31.7% of smokeless tobacco users[19](https://ntcp.mohfw.gov.in/assets/document/surveys-reports-publications/Global-Adult-Tobacco-Survey-Second-Round-India-2016-2017.pdf). This gap highlights the need for more inclusive cessation support.  

The impact of tobacco use is reflected in India’s cancer statistics. According to GLOBOCAN 2024, lip and oral cavity cancer is the most common cancer among Indian men, accounting for 159,734 cases, or 20.4% of all male cancers in men. Lung cancer was the second most common cancer caused with 79,048 cases accounting for 10.1%[21](https://gco.iarc.who.int/today/). The ICMR-NCRP reports mouth and tongue cancers separately, accounting for 8.5% and 5.9% of male cancers, respectively[22](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/static/icmr/dist/images/pdf/reports/Chapter2_Printed.pdf). Together these figures highlight the substantial burden of tobacco-related cancers in Indian men. 

For more on tobacco-related cancer risks, including those associated with areca nut, see the [tobacco and cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/tobacco/) page. 

## Infections, alcohol, weight and diet 

**After tobacco, infections are another important preventable cause of cancer.** Vaccination and, in some cases, effective treatment can reduce the risk of cancers caused by certain infections. 

### Infections: the risk factor Western guidance underweights 

Several viruses and one bacterium can cause cancer, and infection-related cancers account for a larger share of cancers in India than in many high-income countries[23](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805198/). 

* **Human papillomavirus (HPV)** causes almost all [cervical cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/cervical-cancer/) and a substantial share of oropharyngeal, anal, vulvar, [vaginal](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/vaginal-and-vulvar-cancer/) and penile cancers[6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38793561/). Cervical cancer is the second most common cancer in Indian women at 10.2% of female cancers, with 79,360 new cases and 36,319 deaths[24](https://gco.iarc.who.int/today/). India launched a nationwide [HPV vaccination](https://pharmeasy.in/vaccinations) programme on 28 February 2026 at Ajmer, Rajasthan, offering a single dose of quadrivalent Gardasil free at government facilities to approximately 1.15 crore girls aged 14[25](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2). Details, including why one dose is now accepted and what the vaccine does and does not replace, are on the [HPV and cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/hpv/) page.   
* **Hepatitis B and C** cause liver cancer[26](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2). Hepatitis B vaccination is part of India's universal immunisation schedule since 2018, and hepatitis C is manageable with appropriate antiviral treatment[27](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2289761). This means liver cancer risk from both is modifiable.   
* **Helicobacter pylori** causes most gastric cancer and is common in India[28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24587625/). Eradication with antibiotics can help reduce that risk[29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23168695/) .  
* **Epstein-Barr virus** contributes to nasopharyngeal carcinoma and some lymphomas[30](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28893937/).    
* **HIV** raises the risk of several cancers through immune suppression[31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4369071/). 

### Alcohol, weight, activity and diet 

Alcohol has been classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)  as a Group 1 carcinogen (substances, mixtures, and exposure circumstances that the IARC has classified as cancer-causing to humans) since 1988, and WHO's position since January 2023 is that no level of alcohol consumption is safe where cancer risk is concerned[7](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/alcohol-and-cancer),[9](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/04-01-2023-no-level-of-alcohol-consumption-is-safe-for-our-health)[.](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/alcohol-and-cancer) Globally, alcohol was attributed to 741,000 new cancer cases in 2020, 4% of all new cancers[7](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/alcohol-and-cancer),[8](https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/latest-global-data-on-cancer-burden-and-alcohol-consumption). Seven cancer sites are causally linked to alcohol consumption: oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, oesophagus, female breast, liver and colorectum[7](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/alcohol-and-cancer). 

Excess body weight is linked to 13 cancers, including cancers of uterus, oesophagus, stomach, [liver](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/liver-cancer/), [kidney](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/kidney-cancer/), [pancreas](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/pancreatic-cancer/), [gallbladder](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/gallbladder-and-bile-duct-cancer/) colon-rectum, breast, ovaries, [thyroid](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/thyroid-cancer/), multiple myeloma (a type of blood cancer) and meningioma (cancer of layer covering brain and spinal cord)[10](https://www.cdc.gov/cancer/risk-factors/obesity.html),[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9857053/). Physical activity and diet influence cancer risk directly and by affecting body weight[12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37937022/). 

The World Cancer Research Fund and American Institute for Cancer Research package these into a single set of Cancer Prevention Recommendations which includes maintaining a healthy weight, being physically active, eating a diet rich in wholegrains, vegetables, fruit and pulses, limiting red and processed meat, limiting sugar-sweetened drinks and fast food, limiting alcohol, and meeting nutritional needs through food rather than supplements[32](https://www.wcrf.org/research-policy/evidence-for-our-recommendations/wholegrains-veg-fruit-beans/),[33](https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/wcrf-aicr-score/). The [diet, weight and lifestyle](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/lifestyle/) page works through each of these and is about which associations are strong and which are weak. Other such guides available on PharmEasy are [how exercise reduces cancer risk](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/can-exercise-reduce-the-risk-of-cancer/) and [the link between obesity and cancer risk](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/uncover-the-link-between-obesity-and-cancer-risk/). 

## What you cannot change, and why it still matters 

Age is the strongest single risk factor for most cancers[13](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/age). Sex determines which cancers are possible and which are common[18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6029678/). Ethnicity and family history are not modifiable[14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40880847/). 

Inherited risk is the category that most often gets misunderstood. Most cancers are not inherited, but about 5 to 10% of breast cancers and 10 to 15% of ovarian cancers are hereditary. These are often linked to inherited pathogenic variants in genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2, and the mismatch repair genes  associated Lynch syndrome (an inherited genetic condition that increases the risk of several cancers)[14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40880847/),[15](https://www.cdc.gov/breast-ovarian-cancer-hereditary/causes/index.html). While inherited risk cannot be changed, it can be managed through earlier or more frequent screening and, in some cases, risk-reducing medication or surgery[16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3272175/),[34](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36837501/). Our guide on [whether cancer is a genetic disease and when genetic testing is worthwhile](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/diagnostic-is-cancer-a-genetic-disease-hereditary-risk-genetic-testing-guide/) will help you understand this better. 

The same applies to benign disease. Most benign tumours have no known cause and do not turn into cancer[35](https://medlineplus.gov/benigntumors.html). Some cancers, such as Wilms tumour (a kidney tumour in children), are linked to genetic and developmental changes rather than lifestyle[36](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33747498/). These conditions cannot be prevented through lifestyle changes. 

### Screening is the other half of prevention 

Prevention aims to stop cancer from developing[37](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/patient-prevention-overview-pdq). On the other hand, screening looks for cancer, or changes that could become cancer, before symptoms appear. For certain cancers like cervical and colorectal cancer, removing these early changes may restrict cancer from developing[38.](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening/screening-tests) 

India's national programme running under NP-NCD through Ayushman Arogya Mandirs covers screening for oral, breast and cervical cancers from the age of 30 years[39](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2152535). Cervical screening is done using the VIA technique (visual inspection with acetic acid) for women aged 30 to 65, and oral cavity and breast screening by direct inspection and examination. On 26 July 2025, the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare reported that more than 10.18 crore women had been screened as of 20 July 2025[40](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2148754). However, participation in individual screening practices is very less. NFHS-5 (2019 to 21\) reported that only 0.9% of Indian women between the ages of 30 to 49 had a breast examination[41](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39629359/). WHO's Global Status Report 2026 also classifies India as having no organised breast cancer screening programme[42](https://www.who.int/teams/noncommunicable-diseases/ncds-management/cancer-programme/global-status-report-on-cancer-2026). 

India does not have a national programme for colorectal, lung or prostate cancer[43](https://japi.org). The [screening](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/screening/) page explains the broader picture, while our guide to [preventing breast cancer in women](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-how-to-prevent-breast-cancer-in-women/) discusses what to do in the meantime. India's national programme runs under NP-NCD through Ayushman Arogya Mandirs and covers three cancers (oral, breast and cervical) from age 30\. Cervical screening uses visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA) in women aged 30 to 65, and the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare stated on 26 July 2025 that over 10.18 crore women had been screened as of 20 July 2025[40](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2148754). Coverage of individual screening behaviours nevertheless remains very low: NFHS-5 (2019 to 21\) found that only 0.9% of Indian women aged 30 to 49 had ever had a breast examination[41](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39629359/), and WHO's Global Status Report 2026 classifies India as having no organised breast cancer screening programme[44](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/cancer-care-in-india-reading-the-who-global-status-report-2026). 

There is no national programme here for colorectal, lung or prostate screening. The full picture is on the [screening](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/screening/) page, and our note on [preventing breast cancer in women](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-how-to-prevent-breast-cancer-in-women/) covers what to do in the meantime. 

**Where people go outside the government programme.** As the national programme covers only three cancers, much of the screening undertaken in India happens through privately purchased checkup packages. These packages do not replace organised screening, yet the tumour marker tests included in such packages can support diagnosis, monitor management response and help identify whether cancer has returned. However, no test or package can confirm or rule out cancer on its own[45](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet). A package can provide an initial set of results for a doctor to interpret alongside a physical examination.

## Sorting real risks from rumours 

There are several claims made with regard to cancer prevention, however, claims without supporting evidence may distract one from actually useful advice. Common examples of these unsupported claims involve mobile phone use certain cosmetics like sunscreen, deodorant, etc, and the belief that a biopsy can spread cancer. The chance of cancer spreading because of a surgery or a biopsy is extremely low[46](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/myths). The belief can still cause harm, as fear of a biopsy is a documented reason for delaying assessment of a lump[47](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39723014/). 

Three common household concerns are discussed separately in the following links: [whether an air fryer causes cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/does-air-fryer-cause-cancer-unveiling-the-facts-behind-the-question/), [whether sunscreen causes cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-can-sunscreen-cause-cancer-what-the-research-says/), and [whether a tattoo raises cancer risk](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-can-tattoos-increase-your-risk-of-cancer/). 

The [cancer myths and misinformation](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/myths/) page takes these concerns seriously. It explains where each belief came from before discussing what the evidence shows. You can also check [effective ways to reduce your cancer risk](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/cancer-care-how-to-prevent-cancer-effective-ways-to-reduce-your-cancer-risk/). 

### Building a prevention plan that fits your life 

Prioritise changes by how much they help, rather than how easy they are: 

* If you use tobacco in any form, stopping is one of the most important steps you can take to reduce your cancer risk[48](https://progressreport.cancer.gov/prevention/tobacco).    
* If you are overweight, follow strategies to maintain a healthy weight. Stay physically active, and follow a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer).   
* If you drink alcohol, reducing intake lowers risk, and the risk falls further the longer you remain alcohol-free[7](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/alcohol-and-cancer).    
* If your child nis 14, getting an HPV vaccine is an essential measure to avoid cervical/oropharyngeal cancer.  The vaccine can be taken free of cost at a government facility[25](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2).    
* If you are over 30, ask your nearest Ayushman Arogya Mandir which screening you are eligible for[40](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2148754). If several members in a family have cancer, especially at a young age or have the same type of cancer, genetic counselling may be helpful[49](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet).  

Then start with simple changes. Remember that a routine you can maintain for twenty years is more useful than a perfect plan you give up after three weeks. And constant worry about exposures with little effect on risk can increase anxiety without improving health. 

## When to see a doctor 

**Seek care within days if you notice:** 

* A mouth ulcer, white or red patch, or lump that has not healed after three weeks[50](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/oral-cancer).   
* A lump anywhere in the body that is new, firm, fixed or growing[51](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35704829/).    
* Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause[52](https://www.cancer.gov/types/cervical/symptoms).    
* Blood in stool or urine[53](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/symptoms).    
* A cough or hoarse voice lasting more than three weeks[54](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/spot-cancer-early/cancer-symptoms).   
* Unexplained weight loss[53](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/symptoms).    
* Difficulty swallowing that is getting worse[53](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/symptoms).  

**Contact a doctor within a few weeks** **if:** 

* You may want to discuss support for quitting tobacco[48](https://progressreport.cancer.gov/prevention/tobacco).    
* Check whether screening is due[16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3272175/).   
* Ask about HPV vaccination for a daughter in the eligible age group[25](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2).   
* Discuss a worrying family history[49](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet).    
* You want to stop cancer from developing rather than react to a symptom. 

If visiting a clinic is difficult, an [online doctor consultation](https://pharmeasy.in/online-doctor-consultation/) can help you understand which advice applies to you and what to do first. 

**Note:** The information on this page does not replace assessment of a symptom. Prevention may apply when you are well. Any persistent symptom needs examination. However, it is important to look after your health carefully.

## Frequently asked questions 

 

**▾What is the main reason for cancer?** 

There is no single cause. [Cancer](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/) develops when genetic damage builds up and allows a cell to grow without normal control. This damage comes from a combination of ageing, exposures and chance[13](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/age),[14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40880847/),[15](https://www.cdc.gov/breast-ovarian-cancer-hereditary/causes/index.html),[18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6029678/). In India, the largest identifiable contributor is tobacco, linked to 27% of all cancers2. 

**▾Which viruses cause cancer?** 

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is linked to cervical, oropharyngeal, anal, vulvar, vaginal and penile cancers[33](https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/wcrf-aicr-score/).Hepatitis B and C are linked to liver cancer. Epstein-Barr virus is linked to nasopharyngeal carcinoma and some lymphomas. Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) contributes indirectly by weakening the immune system, and HTLV-1 can also cause cancer[55](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/infectious-agents). The bacterium *Helicobacter pylori* mostly causes gastric cancer and some lymphomas[28](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24587625/),[29](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23168695/). 

**▾ Is the cervical cancer vaccine available free in India?** 

Yes, since 28 February 2026, girls aged 14 have been offered a single dose of the quadrivalent HPV vaccine free at government facilities under the national immunisation programme. Approximately 1.15 crore girls are in the target group[25](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2).[5](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234009&reg=3&lang=2) 

**▾ Can cancer be prevented completely?** 

No, WHO estimates approximately 38% of cancers may currently be stopped from developing, which means that most of them may not be stopped[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer). Reducing exposure to known cancer-causing substances can lower risk, but it cannot remove it completely. 

▾**Does cancer always run in families?** 

No, most people who develop cancer may have no family history of it[56](https://academic.oup.com/hmg/article-abstract/23/22/6112/2900819?redirectedFrom=fulltext). Inherited harmful gene changes account for a minority of cases. A strong family pattern is a reason to seek genetic counselling, rather than proof of hereditary cancer[49](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet).  

▾ **What causes benign tumours?** 

Usually there is no clear cause. Benign tumours develop when normal tissue grows excessively in one area. They are not cancerous, and in the great majority of cases do not turn into cancer. They are removed when they cause symptoms or when the diagnosis is uncertain[35](https://medlineplus.gov/benigntumors.html). 

▾**Where can I find India's national cancer prevention body?** 

You can find it at the National Institute of Cancer Prevention and Research (NICPR), an ICMR institute in Noida.[37](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/patient-prevention-overview-pdq) It works on stopping cancer from developing[37](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/patient-prevention-overview-pdq), screening and registry research[57](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/1708493264_medical_social_worker_30052022.pdf),[58](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/organisations/ministry-and-departments/indian-council-medical-research-icmr/national-institute-cancer-prevention-and-research-nicpr-noida?language=en). Population screening is provided through NP-NCD at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs[40](https://www.pib.gov.in/www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2148754). 

▾ **Is there anything I can eat to prevent cancer?** 

No single food can stop cancer from developing. Your overall eating pattern has a modest effect on risk. Diets rich in wholegrains, vegetables, fruit and pulses with less processed meat, sugary drinks and alcohol are associated with lower risk. Supplements do not replace these eating habits and are not recommended for stopping cancers[59](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002096.htm).
