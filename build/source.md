A suspicious blood test result or lump that needs a biopsy may be scary but it does not automatically mean you have cancer. Many times the lump, suspicious shadow on a scan, or raised blood test parameter may not even be related to cancer. 

Cancer is a condition[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer) that occurs when cells divide uncontrollably. Sometimes, these cells stay restricted to one body part, while at other times, they can invade nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20). In India, the number of new cancer cases was projected to increase from approximately 1.46 million in 2022 to 1.57 million in 2025[3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36510887/). According to the IARC’s Global Cancer Observatory, in 2024, India had approximately 780,822 new cancer cases in males and 781,277 in females. This indicated nearly equal distribution between both the sexes[4](https://gco.iarc.who.int/today/en/fact-sheets-populations/356/india%20).  

This page will help you understand what cancer is, how it is diagnosed, the meaning of test results, and what to expect if further tests are recommended, so you can better understand your results without assuming the worst. 

## What is cancer? 

The cells in our body follow specific patterns of growth and renewal. Some, such as those lining the skin and gut and those in the bone marrow are replaced frequently while other cells such as nerve and heart muscle cells generally divide very little or rarely[5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35859206/).  

These processes of cell division, repair and renewal are regulated by instructions that are present in a cell’s DNA.  

* Cancer begins when mutations/genetic changes in a cell’s DNA disrupt these normal processes.    
* When this happens, a cell may continue dividing when it should normally stop.    
* These cells then escape the signals that would normally trigger their death.    
* As a result, they keep multiplying further and accumulating more genetic changes.    
* This allows them to grow more independently, stimulates the formation of blood vessels to supply this growing mass (tumour), and eventually escape from the immune system.    
* Eventually, some of these cancer cells may invade nearby tissues and, in certain cases, spread to other parts of the body (metastasis)[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20).

It is important to note that this process usually takes years and sometimes even decades, as cancer develops through the gradual accumulation of genetic changes. So, a single mutation is rarely enough to cause cancer[6](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030225). This gradual development also creates opportunities for prevention and early detection, allowing many cancers to be prevented or identified before they progress to advanced disease. 

## How does cancer start? 

Cancer develops when changes in the cell’s DNA (genetic changes or mutations) affect the normal processes that regulate cell growth, division, repair and death. These changes may be caused by DNA damage from factors such as tobacco smoke, ultraviolet radiation, chronic infections (like human Papillomavirus \[HPV\] and hepatitis), alcohol, or exposure to chemicals. Family history of cancer accounts for a small percentage of cases. Whether [genetic testing](https://pharmeasy.in/blog/diagnostic-is-cancer-a-genetic-disease-hereditary-risk-genetic-testing-guide/) is worth considering depends on the primary site of cancer and family history[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer),[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20)

Now let us see how these changes happen. Three types of genes play an important role in controlling cell growth: proto-oncogenes, tumour suppressor genes, and DNA repair genes.  

* The first type of genes, proto-oncogenes, help cells grow and divide normally when needed. However, when these genes are mutated or become abnormally active, they can turn into oncogenes, which cause cells to grow and multiply abnormally.    
* The second type of genes, tumour suppressor genes, normally slow down or stop cell growth and division. Mutations that switch off these genes can remove this control and allow abnormal cells to keep dividing without any check.    
* The third type of genes, DNA repair genes, help detect and repair damage to DNA. When DNA repair genes are damaged, the damage to DNA may go unrepaired and starts accumulating as cells divide. This increases the chances of further changes that can contribute to cancer[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20).

These specific genetic changes involved might vary between cancers and even between different cells within the same tumour.

## How are cancers classified? 

Cancer may be classified in several ways, but two of the main approaches are based on where the cancer started and the type of cells or tissue from which it developed, while the third type is based on the molecular profile of the cancer cells. 

### **Classification by primary site** 

A cancer may be named according to the primary site, which is the part of the body where it first developed. For example, cancers may be referred to as breast cancer, prostate cancer, skin cancer, or colon cancer[9](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/disease/categories/site.html%20). This helps identify where the cancer originated. 

### **Classification by histological type** 

Classification is also done based on the type of tissue or cells in which it originated. This is known as its histological type. Major types are mentioned in the table below[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20),[10](https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/patient/melanoma-treatment-pdq%20),[11](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/disease/categories/classification.html%20): 

| Cancer type  | Where it develops  | Common examples  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Carcinomas  | Develops from epithelial cells that form the skin and the lining or covering of many organs and body passages.  | Adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma  |
| Sarcomas  | Develop from connective or supportive tissues such as bone, muscle, cartilage, fat, and other soft tissues.  | Osteosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma  |
| Myelomas  | Develop from plasma cells in the bone marrow.  | Multiple myeloma  |
| [Leukaemias](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/blood-cancer/leukaemia/)  | Develop in tissues involved in the production of blood cells (like bone marrow).  | Lymphocytic/lymphoblastic leukaemia, myeloid leukaemia  |
| [Lymphomas](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/blood-cancer/lymphoma/)  | Develop from cells of the lymphatic system (network of tissues, vessels, and organs in the body).  | Hodgkin lymphoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma  |
| [Melanomas](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/types/skin-cancer/melanoma/)  | Develop from melanocytes (the cells that produce pigment) found primarily in the skin.  | Cutaneous melanoma  |
| Mixed or other types  | Contain features of more than one histological category or have mixed cellular characteristics.  | Adenosquamous carcinoma, mixed mesodermal tumour, carcinosarcoma, teratocarcinoma  |

 

### **Classification by Molecular Profile** 

Cancer can also be classified based on its molecular profile, including specific genetic mutations and proteins present in cancer cells. These features can help doctors identify the cancer subtype, estimate how it may progress or respond to treatment, and also select targeted treatments. Some examples of cancer classification based on molecular markers include[12](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-list%20): 

* **Breast cancer:** ER and PR hormone receptor status and HER2 status help guide treatment.   
* **Lung cancer:** EGFR gene mutations can help doctors select targeted treatments.   
* **Colorectal cancer:** KRAS gene mutations and microsatellite instability (MSI) can help guide treatment decisions.   
* **Melanoma:** BRAF mutations can help determine whether targeted therapy may be suitable.   
* **Leukaemia:** The BCR-ABL fusion gene helps confirm diagnosis and guide targeted treatment. 

## How does cancer spread? 

Cancer spreads when some cells from the primary cancer location invade adjacent areas or travel to another part of the body and form a secondary site (metastases). The cells spread mainly in the following ways[13](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/what-is-cancer/how-cancer-start-grow-spread/how-cancer-can-spread):  

* **Direct spread:** This occurs when cancer cells grow from the original tumour into nearby tissues or organs.   
* **Through the blood:** Cancer cells can enter small blood vessels and circulate through the blood. Some may become trapped in small blood vessels in another organ, move into the tissue in that organ and begin to grow.   
* **Through the lymphatic system:** Cancer cells can enter nearby lymphatic vessels and travel to lymph nodes. Some may be destroyed there, while others can survive and multiply, causing lymph nodal spread. 

**Note:** The secondary cancer is still named after the primary cancer and not the organ where it has spread. For example, if bowel cancer spreads to the liver, it is called bowel cancer with liver metastases, rather than liver cancer, because the cancer cells in the liver originated from the bowel.

## Common symptoms of cancer 

Cancer can cause many symptoms. They usually depend on the type of cancer and where it develops.  

Common symptoms may include unexplained weight loss, fatigue, unexplained lumps or swellings, persistent pain, changes to the skin, a cough or hoarseness that doesn't go away, unexplained bleeding, changes to bowel or bladder habits, and weight loss or night sweats along with a fever[15](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/diagnosis-staging/signs-and-symptoms-of-cancer.html%20). 

**Note:** These symptoms can have many causes other than cancer. However, symptoms that are persistent, unexplained, or worsening should be discussed with a doctor. 

See [cancer symptoms and early signs](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/symptoms/) for details  

## How is cancer diagnosed? 

Sometimes screening tests for cancer may be confused with diagnostic tests. Both are useful in diagnosing cancer but their purpose is slightly different.  

Screening tests are used to look for cancer before symptoms develop (when it may be easier to treat successfully)[16](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening/screening-tests). We will discuss about these in detail in the screening and prevention section. 

Diagnostic tests, on the other hand, are done when symptoms or screening results suggest that cancer may be present. Doctors may use a combination of medical history, physical examination, laboratory tests (markers such as [CEA](https://pharmeasy.in/diagnostics/tests/cea-46), [PSA](https://pharmeasy.in/diagnostics/tests/psa-for-men-119), and [CA 125](https://pharmeasy.in/diagnostics/tests/ca-125-for-women-36)), imaging scans, and other procedures (like a biopsy where a tissue sample is tested) for this purpose[17](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis). See [cancer tests and tumour markers](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/tests/) and [cancer screening](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/screening/) for more details.

## Staging, grading and why they differ 

The stage of cancer describes how large a cancer lesion is and how far it has spread. For example[18](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/staging): 

* The TNM system is the most common system used for the staging of many types of cancer.    
* T stands for the size and spread of the main tumour    
* N refers to whether and how far cancer has spread to the nearby lymph nodes   
* M indicates whether cancer has metastasised to other parts of the body.    
* Stages are typically numbered 0 to IV where    
* Stages I to III refer to increasing tumour size or infiltration into surrounding tissues or lymph nodes.   
* Stage IV indicates that cancer has spread to distant parts of the body. 

The grade of a tumour is based on how abnormal the cancer cells are. A pathologist usually looks at a biopsy sample to determine the grade of a tumour. Cancer cells that resemble normal cells are referred to as well differentiated, whereas cancerous cells that are less like normal cells are called poorly differentiated. Poorly differentiated cells, also known as undifferentiated, are more aggressive and malignant than well differentiated cancer cells. Although different cancers have different grading systems, most utilise Grade 1 to Grade 4 classification, with Grade 1 being the least aggressive and Grade 4 being the most aggressive cancer cells[19](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-grade%20). 

## How is cancer treated? 

The treatment or combination of treatments chosen to fight cancer depends on the type of cancer, how advanced it is, and other factors specific to the individual[20](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types%20). 

| Treatment option  | What it does  |
| :---- | :---- |
| [Surgery](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/surgery/)  | Removes the cancer tumour/tissue from its location and may also remove surrounding tissue if needed (such as lymph nodes).  |
| [Chemotherapy](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/chemotherapy/)  | Uses medicines to kill cancer cells or slow their growth.  |
| [Radiotherapy](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/radiation-therapy/)  | Uses high energy radiation to kill cancer cells or shrink tumours.  |
| [Immunotherapy](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/immunotherapy/)  | Helps the immune system recognise and attack cancer cells.  |
| [Targeted therapy](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/targeted-therapy/)  | Targets specific proteins in cancer cells that help them grow or spread.  |
| [Hormone therapy](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/treatment/hormone-therapy/)  | Blocks or lowers hormones that some cancers need to grow.  |
| Stem cell transplant  | Replaces stem cells, which can help replenish blood cells and immune system after intensive cancer treatment.  |
| Hyperthermia  | Uses heat to damage and kill cancer cells.  |

## **What to check for insurance in India** 

Coverage depends on your specific policy, insurer, hospital, and policy terms. You can use the following points as questions to discuss with your insurer before treatment begins. 

| What to ask about  | Why it matters for cancer treatment  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Does your health insurance cover cancer?**  | A standard health policy reimburses inpatient hospital expenses and may not cover expenses related to cancer care. A cancer-specific plan usually pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a defined stage. Both are different and should be discussed with the provider for exact coverage details.  |
| **Day-care treatment**  | Chemotherapy and radiotherapy are usually delivered without an overnight stay. Some insurance companies may list and cover them under day-care procedures. So, confirm that your specific procedure is on the insurer’s day-care list.  |
| **Pre- and post-hospitalisation**  | Scans, biopsies and blood work before admission and follow-up after discharge are covered only for a limited number of days and usually only when linked to the same claim. So, make sure to keep every receipt and check with the insurer beforehand for any specific timeline for claims.  |
| **Waiting periods**  | A cancer diagnosed before the policy started or within an initial waiting period is normally not covered. Pre-existing disease and specific-illness waiting periods differ from each other. So, check the exact wording in your policy schedule.  |
| **Sub-limits, co-pay and room rent**  | A room-rent cap can reduce the whole claim, not just the room charge. Sub-limits on specific procedures and a co-pay percentage can also influence your actual claim.   |
| **Cashless or reimbursement**  | Cashless works only at some hospitals and needs pre-authorisation, usually a specific number of hours ahead, for planned admissions. Outside the network, you pay first and claim after.  |
| **Documents to keep from day one**  | Policy number and card, doctor’s prescription and treatment plan, the histopathology and staging reports, discharge summary, itemised hospital bill, pharmacy invoices and diagnostic reports. Missing histopathology is the most common reason a cancer claim may get delayed.  |

 

Financial support for cancer treatment in India may be available through schemes such as Ayushman Bharat PMJAY, the Health Minister’s Cancer Patient Fund under the Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi (RAN), and government measures such as National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) price controls on certain anti-cancer medicines[38](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1738169&reg=48&lang=2).  

However, the coverage depends on the scheme’s eligibility rules and approved treatment packages, not on the hospital alone[38](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1738169&reg=48&lang=2). Verified as of {{CURRENT\_MONTH\_YEAR}}.

## Screening and prevention 

The prevalence of cancer screening in India remains relatively low. Data from the National Family Health Survey 5 found that only 0.9% of women aged 30 to 49 years had undergone breast cancer screening, while 1.9% had undergone cervical cancer screening[39](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39629359/). Further details, including regional variation and the existing schemes, are listed in [Cancer in India](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/india/). 

Screening recommendations depend on the type of cancer, age, and individual risk factors. In India, population-based screening is recommended for oral, cervical, and breast cancers among people aged 30 to 65 years. 

Oral cancer screening involves an oral visual examination (OVE), while cervical cancer screening uses visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA), and breast cancer screening involves a clinical breast examination (CBE). Each is recommended once every five years[40](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-03/Operational%20Framework%20Management%20of%20Common%20Cancers.pdf). 

Additionally, those with significant risk factors, such as tobacco use, a relevant family history, or certain chronic infections, should discuss with a doctor whether earlier or additional screening is appropriate[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer),[16](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening/screening-tests). 

If you have no symptoms but would like to get some screening tests done, PharmEasy Diagnostics offers a [cancer screening package for men](https://pharmeasy.in/diagnostics/packages/cancer-screening-male-1420) and a [cancer screening package for women](https://pharmeasy.in/diagnostics/packages/cancer-screening-female-1418). These packages include commonly used markers and basic tests; however, they do not replace recommended government screening programmes or confirm or rule out cancer on their own.  

Cancer prevention means taking steps to reduce the risk of developing cancer. The WHO estimates that around 38% of cancers can currently be prevented[1](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/cancer).

 In India, important prevention measures include avoiding tobacco in all forms, getting HPV and hepatitis B vaccinations, and lifestyle changes such as including fruits and vegetables in diet, limiting alcohol, minimising UV exposure/radiations, and maintaining a healthy weight and regular physical activity[41](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41778182/). Read [cancer prevention](https://pharmeasy.in/cancer/prevention/) to know more. 

## When to see a doctor? 

Consult a doctor if you notice symptoms that persist, worsen, or keep coming back. These may include a new lump or swelling, unexplained weight loss, persistent tiredness, unusual bleeding or bruising, changes in bowel or bladder habits, a persistent cough, a changing mole, or difficulty swallowing. These symptoms may have causes other than cancer, but they should be evaluated to identify the cause[42](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cancer/%20).

Seek urgent medical attention for serious symptoms such as coughing up blood, seeing blood in your pee, a painful lump, a mouth sore that does not heal, or difficulty breathing, or any other symptom that you are concerned about[15](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/diagnosis-staging/signs-and-symptoms-of-cancer.html%20),[42](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cancer/%20). Do not wait for a routine appointment if symptoms are severe or rapidly worsening. 

If you are unsure whether a symptom needs medical attention, an [online doctor consultation](https://pharmeasy.in/online-doctor-consultation/) can help you understand whether you should get it checked and how urgently you may need further evaluation.

## Frequently Asked Questions 

### ▾ What is cancer in simple words? 

Cancer is a disease in which abnormal cells lose the normal controls that regulate their growth and division. These cells can continue multiplying, invade nearby tissues and in some cases spread to distant parts of the body[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20).

### ▾ Is a tumour the same as cancer? 

A tumour is an unusual lump or mass of tissue that may be benign or malignant. Benign tumours are not cancer and usually stay in the same place. Malignant tumours are cancerous and can grow into nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20).

### ▾ What is the difference between a cancer cell and a normal cell? 

A normal cell destroys itself if it is damaged beyond repair. Cancer cells; however, differ from normal cells as they continue to proliferate, ignore signals to stop or die, invade nearby tissues, spread to other parts of the body, develop their own blood supply, and avoid destruction by the immune system[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20).

### ▾ What does metastatic cancer mean? 

Metastatic cancer means the cancer has spread from its primary location to a distant organ[2](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer%20). It will still be named as per the original cancer. For example, breast cancer that spreads to the bones is metastatic breast cancer, not bone cancer. 

### ▾ Does cancer always cause pain? 

Most cancers are painless at the beginning. Pain usually develops later, when a tumour presses on a nerve or affects the bone[43](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cancer/%20).

### ▾ Can cancer be cured? 

Some cancers can be cured, especially when found and treated early. Please note that the chances of cure depend on the cancer type, stage, and response to treatment. Sometimes after successful treatment, doctors may say “the cancer is in remission” as it may return[44](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205135/%20).

### ▾ Does a biopsy make cancer spread? 

 The risk of a biopsy causing cancer to spread is extremely low. Biopsies are routinely performed to diagnose cancer and the benefits of getting an accurate diagnosis generally far outweigh this risk[45](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/understanding-cancer/can-cancer-be-cured.html).
